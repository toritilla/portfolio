    Case study · UXDG 101

    Flora & Fauna: Reimagined

    F&F: Reimagined was a team project created in my UXDG 101 class. It was a learning process and my first real experience with user experience design.

    Overview

    Neighborhood bakery, coffee house, and supper club.

    An outdated website can make a great cafe hard to find and harder to love. Flora & Fauna: Reimagined breathes new life into a beloved local Savannah cafe in a visually appealing, user-friendly way. Shaped by grounded user research and thoughtful design, the redesign makes the cafe experience effortless for its customers.

    My role
    Interaction Designer
    Course
    UXDG 101, User Experience Design Methods
    Team
    Evelyn Swanson, Evie Wang, Tori Ludwig, Amy Sim, Evie Herb
    Methods
    Observational study, card sorting, wireframing, prototyping

    The problem

    When visiting the Flora & Fauna website, customers could easily get confused while trying to navigate it. The site lacked information, its text was hard to read, and its navigation buttons were misleading. That experience could deter new patrons from visiting, costing the cafe business in the long run.

    Our solution

    We streamlined the site around what customers want to know: reservations, menus, the Flora & Fauna story, and online ordering, each named clearly. Anyone left with questions can reach the cafe through an organized footer. The result makes regulars feel appreciated and opens the doors to new faces.

    Discover

    Research

    We conducted a “fly on the wall” observational study at Flora & Fauna to learn about the customer base, the overall aesthetic, and the company’s values.

    Research logistics

    We sat at a good vantage point to observe the atmosphere of the cafe, as well as customer and employee interactions, on different days.

    We chose three guiding questions so we could stay focused on our goal throughout the discover and develop phases.

    Questions we focused on

    1. How might we better cater to the demographic and needs of our users?
    2. How might we identify specific elements that Flora & Fauna is lacking compared to other restaurants and cafes?
    3. How might we better reflect the mission and values of Flora & Fauna?

    From our primary and secondary research, we pulled out the core findings that would guide the next phases of the project.

    Core findings

    Friendly & welcoming

    • Staff and customer interactions
    • Relaxed atmosphere
    • Slow paced

    Visual brand

    • Consistent aesthetic
    • Color palette

    Sophisticated & sustainable

    • Interior design
    • High-end menu items
    • Reusable products

    Customer base

    • Tourists
    • Locals
    • All ages

    We sorted out which parts of the original site were working, and which could be a source of frustration for users.

    Working
    • Well-received aesthetic
    • In-person experience
    • Color palette
    • Product photos
    Pain points
    • Disorganized
    • Random or unnecessary links
    • Lack of information
    • Brand representation

    Final design decisions

    Look through the final decisions we made to see how we curated our redesign and built Flora & Fauna: Reimagined.

    • Increased accessibility

      We tested the cafe’s iconic palette against WCAG standards and tweaked hues for readability, then added clear hover feedback so buttons and photos show they can be selected.

    • Restructured IA

      Using our research, mainly a card sorting exercise, we reorganized the navigation and renamed pages to reflect what they hold.

    • Clear navigation

      Clearer categories and fewer, more condensed pages reduce cognitive load, with some pages combined into one.

    • Personal touches

      Engaging media and personal stories about the Flora & Fauna family bring the homely feel of the cafe online.

    Results

    Outcomes & prototype

    Traceability

    Accessibility & user needs

    • Adjusted the color palette and typography hierarchy so the site is accessible to all users and follows WCAG standards
    • Clear, consistent menus

    Misleading navigation buttons

    • Content under the navigation drop-downs is aptly named and categorized

    Lack of information about company values

    • Added the story of how Flora & Fauna started, with dedicated pages for About information

    Outcomes

    Increased accessibility

    • A newly adjusted color palette and typography hierarchy
    • Follows WCAG standards
    • Customers can find context about Flora & Fauna’s values
    • Nobody has to make assumptions or feel excluded and confused

    Reduced cognitive load

    • The original navigation was not intuitive
    • Drop-down content is aptly named and categorized, reducing cognitive load for all users
    • Clear, consistent menus are essential
    • Menus are separated by time of day and type of food or drink, with an emphasis on cafe service versus dinner service

    Impact

    Flora & Fauna: Reimagined has a clear impact. With its increased accessibility, clarity, and detail, the site gives every visitor a consistent, sincere experience that reflects the cafe itself. It aims to meet the needs of current and future customers and set a higher standard for competitors.

    Project · Figma Make

    PassioGo Redesign with Figma Make

    Location
    Savannah, GA
    Role
    UX Designer
    Software
    Figma Make

    This piece was an experiment with Figma Make when it first came out. I made a prototype for a revised PassioGo app, the shuttle tracker SCAD students use, using insights and pain points from the current app to improve it.

    The problem

    Pain points in the current app

    • Inaccurate timing

      Bus arrival times on the routes weren’t reliable.

    • Hard to find stops

      It was difficult to see which routes serve a specific stop.

    • Hard to plan trips

      It was difficult to work out which routes to take to get where you want to go.

    Reflection

    Lessons from vibe coding

    What I learned

    • Giving the tool information up front, before any coding starts, is valuable and saves time.
    • Specific language is needed to get certain objectives implemented.
    • Figma Make and vibe coding can do a lot, but they struggle with very specific, nuanced goals, like drawing the routes on the map.

    What I’d improve next time

    • Provide even more information up front.
    • Learn more coding techniques so I can make changes myself without typing a prompt.
    • Put more information into a single prompt to speed up the process.
